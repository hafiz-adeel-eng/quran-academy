## Body (paste into Post Content)

What is Tajweed? Simply put, it is the set of rules for reciting the Quran correctly — pronouncing every letter from its proper point of articulation, giving it its due characteristics, and applying the rules that make recitation beautiful. The word itself comes from an Arabic root meaning "to make something good or beautiful."

Tajweed matters because the Quran was revealed with a precise way of being read. Getting it right is how we honour the Book we recite. Getting it wrong — even unknowingly — can change the meaning of words entirely.

### Why Tajweed matters
Reading the Quran without Tajweed is like reading a musical score without rhythm: technically letters, but not the recitation it was revealed to be. Tajweed preserves two things: correctness of meaning and beauty of sound.

### The main rules you'll learn
A Tajweed course builds up rule by rule. The core areas:

- **Makharij (points of articulation)** — where each letter is produced in the mouth and throat
- **Sifaat (characteristics of letters)** — qualities like heaviness, lightness, clarity and softness
- **Rules of Noon and Meem** — how to handle letters like Ikhfa, Idgham and Iqlab when they appear together
- **Qalqalah** — the small "echo" given to certain letters
- **Madd (elongation)** — how long to stretch particular vowel sounds
- **Waqf (stopping)** — where and how to stop when pausing during recitation

### Do I need to understand Arabic?
No. Tajweed is about how the sounds are made, not their meaning. Many students learn full Tajweed without knowing a word of Arabic — they are taught through practice and listening, with rules explained clearly in English.

### How long does it take to learn Tajweed?
With 3–5 lessons a week, most students complete the full set of rules in 6–12 months, then spend time applying the rules by reciting regularly with a teacher. The key is consistency — ten minutes of daily practice beats a two-hour weekly session every time.

### Can you learn Tajweed online?
Yes — online is arguably the best way. In a 1-on-1 video lesson, your teacher hears every sound clearly and corrects you in real time. No travel, no scheduling conflicts, and you can choose a teacher (including a female teacher) that fits your family.
