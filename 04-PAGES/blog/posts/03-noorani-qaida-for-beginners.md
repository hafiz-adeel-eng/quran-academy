## Body (paste into Post Content)

Noorani Qaida is the perfect starting point for beginners of any age. It is a small, structured book that teaches the Arabic alphabet, pronunciation, and how letters join into words — the essential foundation before reading the Quran itself. If you cannot yet read a single Arabic word, this is where you start.

### What is Noorani Qaida?
Developed to make the Quran accessible to non-Arabic speakers, Noorani Qaida presents Arabic in gradual, repeatable steps. Each page builds on the last, so there is no jumping ahead — you master letters, then sounds, then words, then reading.

### What you'll learn
Working through the Qaida with a teacher, you'll cover:

- **The 28 Arabic letters** — recognising each letter's shape at the start, middle and end of words
- **Makharij (points of pronunciation)** — where each letter is produced in the mouth and throat
- **Harakat (vowels)** — Fatha, Kasra and Damma, the short vowel marks
- **Sukoon** — the "no-vowel" stop that appears in almost every verse
- **Tanween** — the doubled vowels (double-Fatha, etc.)
- **Madd (elongation)** — stretching sounds for the correct length
- **Joining letters into words** — the step that turns sounds into actual reading

### How long does Noorani Qaida take?
Most students complete the Qaida in 3–6 months with 3–5 lessons a week. Young children and busy adults progress at their own pace — the book is designed so no step is skipped, so the foundation is always solid.

### How to start Noorani Qaida online
1. **Book a free trial class** — a certified teacher assesses your starting point in 30 minutes.
2. **Follow the Qaida page by page** — in 1-on-1 lessons the teacher corrects every letter in real time.
3. **Practise a few minutes daily** — consistency beats long, rare sessions.
4. **Finish into Nazra** — once you read fluently, move to Nazra (Quran reading) or begin Tajweed.

### Tips for beginners
- Choose a teacher who corrects your Makharij early — habits form fast
- For children, keep sessions short (20–30 minutes) and celebrate small wins
- Request a female teacher if that makes you or your child more comfortable
- Don't rush — a slow, correct Qaida beats a fast, incorrect one
