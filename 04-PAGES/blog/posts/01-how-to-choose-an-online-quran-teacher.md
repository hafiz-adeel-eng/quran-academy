## Body (paste into Post Content)

How to choose an online Quran teacher is the most important decision you'll make on your Quran journey — and the easiest to get wrong if you rush. The right teacher makes learning joyful and fast; the wrong one can set back a child's confidence for months. In this guide we share the six checks our own families use, the same standards QuranlyHub applies to every teacher before they teach.

### 1. Check their certification (Ijazah)
An Ijazah is a certification from a qualified teacher confirming the holder can recite and teach the Quran correctly. Without it, a tutor is an enthusiast, not a qualified teacher. Always ask to see the certificate — and verify it, not just accept a name drop.

### 2. Verify background and identity
Teaching children in your home (even online) deserves the same caution as any childcare. A reputable academy background-checks every teacher and verifies references. If a tutor or academy can't confirm this, move on.

### 3. Assess teaching style with a free trial
Never commit to a paid plan before meeting the teacher. A free trial class tells you in 30 minutes what a hundred website reviews cannot: do they listen? Are they patient? Do they adapt to your child's level? Most good academies offer a free trial — ours does.

### 4. Consider gender preference
Many families — especially sisters — prefer a female Quran teacher. Make sure you can request one and that it's honoured. A comfort in learning is not a luxury; it's part of the method.

### 5. Check timings and timezone fit
The best teacher is useless if they're always awake when you're asleep. Confirm the teacher or academy covers your timezone with regular weekly slots — 7 days a week is the standard to expect.

### 6. Look at progress reporting
Good teaching is measurable. Choose an academy that sends monthly progress reports and provides certificates at each level. If a teacher can't tell you what was covered last month, they're not tracking anything.

### Red flags to avoid
- No verifiable Ijazah or certification
- No background checks or references
- No free trial, or pressure to pay upfront
- No progress reports or lesson records
- Only group classes when you asked for 1-on-1

### What if you pick wrong?
It happens — the fit matters. Choose a provider that replaces a teacher free, no questions asked, so you're never stuck paying for lessons that don't work.
